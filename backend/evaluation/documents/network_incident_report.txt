Post-Incident Review: INC-2024-0917
Severity 1. Customer-facing checkout degradation.

Summary

On 17 September 2024 the checkout service returned elevated error rates for 74
minutes. Approximately 11 percent of checkout attempts failed during the window.
No customer data was lost and no payment was double-captured. The incident was
declared at 14:12 UTC and resolved at 15:26 UTC.

Timeline

13:58 UTC - A routine deployment shipped a change to the order-history query.
14:04 UTC - Checkout p99 latency crossed 2 seconds; the automated alert fired.
14:12 UTC - The on-call engineer declared INC-2024-0917 and paged the database
team under the standing severity-1 escalation path.
14:31 UTC - The team identified saturated connection pools on the primary.
14:49 UTC - The deployment was rolled back. Latency began recovering immediately.
15:26 UTC - Error rates returned to baseline and the incident was closed.

Root cause

The shipped query dropped a predicate on tenant_id. Without it the planner
abandoned the composite index on the orders table and fell back to a sequential
scan of 84 million rows. Each checkout therefore held a connection far longer
than budgeted, the pool of 200 connections saturated, and requests queued until
they timed out. The failure was not a capacity problem: the same hardware had
served triple this traffic the previous week.

Why detection was slow

The alert that fired was a latency threshold, not a saturation alert. Connection
pool utilisation was collected but had no alert bound to it, so the six minutes
between deployment and page were spent on a symptom rather than a cause. The
deployment itself emitted no marker into the observability timeline, which is
why correlating the change with the alert took a further nineteen minutes.

Contributing factors

The change passed review because the removed predicate looked redundant with a
tenant scope applied at the application layer. Staging carried 60 thousand rows,
so the sequential scan there completed in under a millisecond and nothing in the
test suite asserted on the query plan.

Action items

platform team. Due 1 October 2024.
AI-2: Emit deployment markers into the observability timeline. Owner: release
engineering. Due 8 October 2024.

AI-4: Load staging to at least 10 percent of production row counts. Owner:
