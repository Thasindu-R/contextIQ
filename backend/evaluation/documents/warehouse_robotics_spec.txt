AR-4200 Autonomous Warehouse Transport
Technical specification, document number WRS-2024-08.

Overview

The AR-4200 is a low-profile autonomous mobile robot for shelf-to-station
transport in fulfilment centres. It replaces the AR-3100 and is not mechanically
compatible with it: the mast interface changed from a four-bolt to a six-bolt
pattern, and AR-3100 shelving adapters must be retired rather than reused.

Payload and drive

Rated payload is 480 kilograms on a level floor and 310 kilograms on a grade of
up to three degrees. The differential drive delivers 42 newton-metres of
continuous torque per wheel, with a peak of 68 newton-metres available for two
seconds during obstacle recovery. Maximum transit speed is 2.1 metres per second
when carrying a load and 2.8 metres per second when running empty. The unit
stops within 0.9 metres from full speed under emergency braking.

Power and charging

A 48-volt lithium iron phosphate pack provides eight hours of continuous duty.
Opportunity charging at any of the floor contacts brings the pack from 20 to 80
percent in 22 minutes. The fleet controller schedules charging automatically
whenever a unit finishes a task below 35 percent state of charge and no queued
task is waiting, so a healthy fleet never charges on the critical path.

Navigation

Localisation fuses wheel odometry, a 2D safety lidar scanning at 15 hertz, and
ceiling fiducial markers placed every six metres. Ceiling markers are the
authority: when odometry and marker fixes disagree by more than 40 millimetres
the unit halts and requests a manual pose reset rather than guessing. Loss of
marker visibility for longer than 12 seconds triggers the same halt.

Safety envelope

The safety lidar enforces a two-stage field. A human entering the warning field
at 1.8 metres slows the unit to 0.4 metres per second; entering the protective
field at 0.6 metres commands a category-2 stop. Both fields are certified to
ISO 3691-4 and cannot be disabled from the fleet controller. Field muting is
permitted only inside marked charging bays.

Maintenance intervals

Drive-wheel polyurethane tyres are replaced every 2000 operating hours or at 3
millimetres of remaining tread, whichever comes first. The lidar window is
cleaned weekly; a soiled window is the single most common cause of spurious
protective stops. Battery packs are capacity-tested annually and retired at 80
percent of nameplate.

Fault codes

Fault E-114 indicates a marker-disagreement halt and clears after a manual pose
reset. Fault E-207 indicates drive-wheel slip beyond the traction model and
usually means a fluid spill on the floor. Fault E-330 is a charging-contact
overtemperature and locks the bay until an engineer inspects it.
